# OpenFreeyond: a fenced garden with a tiled path to the street

## Problem Statement

The garden does not look enclosed, and the path leads nowhere. Today a person walking toward the edge of the place is stopped by an invisible wall, with no fence in front of them. Only two short fences exist (a left and a right one, each 10 m long, standing inside the garden), so most of the edge has nothing visible that blocks the way. The path from the house door runs over a gravel texture and ends in open grass at the edge of the place. The existing specs speak of "the street" (the spawn point is "a free spot in the street"), but no street is drawn: there is no sidewalk, no road and no sign that the path connects to anything. The place is meant to feel like a real, cosy home ([G-1 of the companion spec](../openfreeyond-companion/spec.md)); an invisible wall and a path to nowhere work against that.

How we know: one observation, by the builder, of the running app, together with the existing layout data and specs, which show two fences, a texture-only path and no street. No independent viewer has judged it. Nothing has been measured. The problem rests on the builder's own judgement. The PO chose on 2026-11-09 to go ahead on that basis, without independent evidence.

## Goals

- G-1: The garden looks enclosed, so that a person is stopped by something they can see. Metric: the number of the checklist items of R-1 to R-4, R-11 and R-12 that the builder judges met (yes or no for each). Only the builder judges them. Baseline: 0, because only two short fences exist and the rest of the edge is an invisible wall. Target: all items met. Timeframe: before the feature goes online.
- G-2: The path and the street read as a connected, real street scene. Metric: the number of the checklist items of R-5 to R-10, R-13, R-16 and R-17 that the builder judges met (yes or no for each). Only the builder judges them. Baseline: 0, because the path is a texture that ends in open grass and no street is drawn. Target: all items met, except cars and lamps that are listed as missing because no free asset was found (R-16) or that were reduced by the frame-rate rule (R-14), and the shadows dropped by that rule; these exceptions are written down in the checklist. Timeframe: before the feature goes online.
- G-3: The place stays smooth with the new objects. Metric: average frames per second over the 2-minute walk of R-14, read from the browser's built-in frame-rate display. Baseline: not yet measured with the interior, the shadows and these additions. Target: 30 or more in Chrome on the builder's own laptop (the builder names the laptop when writing down the measurement); Firefox and Safari are best effort. The walk always follows the same route (R-14). Timeframe: before the feature goes online.
- G-4: The place stays within the download limit. Metric: compressed transfer size of the first visit with an empty cache, from the start screen through the place, the street, the house and the character, measured with Chrome's network tool with the person standing in the garden (R-15). Baseline: the shipped assets are about 15 MB today (measured from the shipped files on 2026-11-09); the first-visit transfer has not been measured. Target: at most 100 MB (the limit of the house feature). Timeframe: before the feature goes online.

## Requirements

- R-1: A continuous fence stands along all four edges of the place, at the place where the person is stopped today (the current edge, 24 m by 22 m, unless R-11 moves it), so that the fence is what stops the person. A person walking into the fence stops in front of it and cannot pass, as at every other solid object. Goal: G-1. Evidence: PO request and decision, 2026-11-09.
- R-2: The fence keeps the look of today's fences: posts every metre and two rails, 1 m high, cream colour. The two fences that stand inside the garden today are removed from the inside of the garden; they become part of the fence line at the edge. No fence stands inside the garden. A garden object that overlaps the new fence line (for example a tree near the back edge) is moved the smallest distance inward that clears it; nothing is removed. The same rule applies to an object that overlaps the path or its 2 m extension. A moved object never ends on top of another solid object, on the path, or on the spawn point or the character's start spot. Goal: G-1. Evidence: PO decisions, 2026-11-09.
- R-3: The street-side fence has a gate at the end of the path. The gate has a clear opening of 1.2 m between its two posts, as wide as the path, and stands centred on the path. It matches the fence in look and height, with a post on each side of it, and it is the only break in the rhythm of one post every metre. It stays closed and cannot be opened. A person walking into it stops. Goal: G-1. Evidence: PO decision, 2026-11-09.
- R-4: A person never reaches a point where an edge of the place stops them without a fence in front of them. There is no gap in the fence line other than the closed gate, and no part of the edge is open. Goal: G-1. Evidence: PO request, 2026-11-09.
- R-5: The walking path is made of rectangular paving slabs of 60 by 30 cm, laid in rows with visible joints, so that individual tiles can be told apart when the person stands on the path and looks along it. The path is 1.2 m wide (two whole slabs across; today it is 1.4 m). Partial slabs are allowed at the ends of the path and where it meets the sidewalk, but not along its long sides. Goal: G-2. Evidence: PO request and decision, 2026-11-09.
- R-6: The path runs from the front door to the gate, which is about 2 m longer than today (today the path ends 14 m from the door), and continues beyond the gate to meet the sidewalk at the sidewalk's inner edge, so that it looks connected to it. The stretch beyond the gate cannot be walked on (R-9). Goal: G-2. Evidence: PO request and decision, 2026-11-09.
- R-7: Beyond the street-side fence there is a sidewalk made of the same kind of tiles, 1.8 m wide (three whole slabs across) and running along the fence, a curb 15 cm high, and a two-lane road 7 m wide with a centre line. The street runs along the whole street-side fence and carries on out of view at both ends. The person sees it over the fence from the start spot on the path just inside the gate once they turn toward the gate, and from anywhere along the street-side fence. Goal: G-2. Evidence: PO decisions, 2026-11-09.
- R-8: The street is dressed with 3 parked cars at the curb and 3 street lamps along the sidewalk, standing along the street within 10 m either side of the point opposite the gate. They do not move and make no sound, and the lamps are not lit. Each item is judged on its own: an item that stands as a plain block because no free model was found (R-16) is listed as missing and does not count as met. Goal: G-2. Evidence: PO decision, 2026-11-09.
- R-9: The sidewalk, the curb, the road and everything on them cannot be reached or walked on. The person stays in the garden. Goal: G-2. Evidence: PO decision, 2026-11-09.
- R-10: The ground, the sky and the fog still reach beyond the street, so that no empty edge or void shows from anywhere in the garden, including at the ends of the road. Goal: G-2. Evidence: the same rule in the earlier specs.
- R-11: The walkable area outside the house stays at least as large as in phase 1 (444.5 m², measured from the phase 1 layout data with the same grid method as the house feature), with the fence line at the current edge and the footprint of the fence itself counted as covered, like every other solid. If the fence footprint or a moved object would bring the area below 444.5 m², the left, right and back edges of the place, and with them the fence line, move outward until the area is met. The street-side (front) edge does not move, so the gate, the street and the path length of R-6 hold. The character stays in the garden, inside the fence, and his wandering, greeting and waving work as they do today. Goal: G-1. Evidence: PO decision, 2026-11-09, and the rule of the house feature.
- R-12: These things keep working as before: walking and looking, solid objects stopping the person, the front door and the enterable house, the character with his animations and chosen colours, the sun and its shadows, the start screen and the intake. The spawn point is in the garden on the tiled path 2 m inside the gate, free and reachable, and the person faces the house when they enter; the character's start spot stays a free spot that a person can reach. Goal: G-1. Evidence: PO decision, 2026-11-09, and the existing specs.
- R-13: The new objects (fences, gate, tiles, curb, road, cars, lamps) look at home in the sun: they cast and receive shadows like the other objects in the garden. Goal: G-2. Evidence: PO decision, 2026-11-09 (the sun shadows already in the place).
- R-14: Walking averages 30 frames per second or better over a 2-minute walk that always follows the same route: from the start spot along the path to the front door, through every room and back out. If the builder's measurement misses the target, the cars and lamps are reduced first, down to no fewer than 1 car and 1 lamp (never the fence, the tiles or the house), and then the sun shadows on the street objects are dropped. This is decided before the feature goes online, from the builder's measurement; nothing switches at run time. If the walk still misses the target, the feature does not go online until it is met. Goal: G-3. Evidence: PO decision, 2026-11-09 (the current limit is kept).
- R-15: The compressed first-visit transfer, with an empty cache, from the start screen through the place, the house and the character, is at most 100 MB. The measurement is read with the person standing in the garden, once no new file has arrived for 10 seconds. Goal: G-4. Evidence: PO decision, 2026-11-09.
- R-16: Every new asset (the cars, the lamps, the tile, curb and road textures) has a recorded licence (author, source link, licence text, any credit line) that is usable: CC0, CC-BY with the credit shown, or one that explicitly allows use and redistribution. NC, ND or no licence is not usable. Credit lines are shown in the existing Credits list of the start page. Every asset ships with the app and loads from its own address, never from a third-party host. The car models and textures carry no readable brand logos or number plates. The builder must first try to find a free asset for each item. If none can be found, a plain block or colour in its place is acceptable: the feature still ships and the missing items are listed. Goal: G-2. Evidence: the same rule in the earlier specs.
- R-17: If a car or lamp model, or a tile, curb or road texture, fails to load, the place still loads, with a plain block or colour in its place and a short message of one sentence in the place's existing notice area, the same way other load messages are shown. When several assets fail in one load, one message is shown for that load; it is not repeated during the same visit. The fence line and the gate do not depend on a loaded file. Goal: G-2. Evidence: the same rule in the earlier specs (FR-16 of the world-visuals spec).
- R-18: Nothing about a person is stored or sent. This feature collects, stores and sends no personal data. Goal: G-1 and G-2 (a condition that holds for the whole feature). Evidence: NFR-3 of the companion spec.
- R-19: Each of R-1 to R-13, R-16 and R-17 has a short written checklist of concrete things to see, written into the spec's acceptance criteria before building starts, so that the builder can judge it as met or not met. Goal: G-1 and G-2. Evidence: PO decision, 2026-11-09 (success is a checklist, as in the earlier features).

## Scope

- A continuous fence line at the current edge of the place on all four sides, in today's fence look, with the two inner fences moved out to it.
- A closed gate in the street-side fence at the end of the path.
- A path of rectangular 60 by 30 cm paving slabs, 1.2 m wide, from the front door to the gate, about 2 m longer than today, continuing beyond the gate to meet the sidewalk.
- Beyond the fence: a 1.8 m tiled sidewalk, a 15 cm curb, a 7 m road with a centre line, 3 parked cars and 3 street lamps, all static and not reachable.
- The spawn point moves to the tiled path just inside the gate, facing the house.
- Sun shadows on the new objects, licence records and credits for the new assets, and a plain block or colour when an asset is missing.

## Out of Scope

- Opening the gate, or any other new door that opens.
- Walking on the sidewalk or the road, or leaving the garden.
- Moving cars, pedestrians, traffic, animals and any sound.
- Lit street lamps, night, weather and day-night changes.
- Changing the house, the interior, the character, the start screen or the intake.
- Any other change to the garden: trees, bushes, beds and furniture stay where they are, except that an object overlapping the new fence line or path is moved the smallest distance inward that clears it.
- Mobile and touch support.

## Open Questions, Assumptions and Risks

- A-1: The builder and the PO are the same person, as in the earlier features. Only the builder judges the checklists.
- A-2: The current edge of the place is 24 m by 22 m (x from −12 to 12, z from −11 to 11, measured from the layout data on 2026-11-09). The fence line follows whatever the edge is when building starts.
- A-3: Free models with usable licences exist for parked cars and street lamps, and free textures for tiles, curb and asphalt. Not yet sourced. R-16 allows plain blocks or colours if one is not found.
- A-4: The street side is the front edge of the garden, the +z edge (z = 11), the direction the path leads today. Today the path runs from the front door (z = −5) to z = 9, which is 14 m; the gate stands at the edge (z = 11), so the path to the gate is 16 m, about 2 m longer. These figures are measured from the layout data on 2026-11-09.
- A-5: Whether the tiles are separate objects or a drawn pattern is a technical choice for the plan, as long as individual tiles with joints can be told apart (R-5).
- Risk: many fence sections, tiles, cars and lamps cost frame rate. Addressed by: R-14 and G-3. If the target is missed, what to drop first is to be determined from the builder's measurement.
- Risk: the fence line at the current edge puts the fence where trees or other objects may already stand (tree-5 and tree-6 stand near the back edge). Addressed by: R-2 (move the smallest distance inward).
- Risk: moving an object changes where the character can wander. Addressed by: R-11 (he stays in the garden, and the walkable area stays at least 444.5 m²).
- Dependency: the sun-shadow work already in the place (R-13); the existing Credits list and licence checks (R-16); the asset sourcing of A-3; the walkable-area rule of the house feature (R-11).
- Risk: a person looks over the fence at a street that cannot be reached; this is the PO's intended look, and the closed gate is the visible reason.
- Open question: none that is a product decision. Technical questions for the plan: how the tiles are built (A-5), how far the shadows must reach over the street (dependency above), and how the gate and the fence line are measured for the 444.5 m² check.
