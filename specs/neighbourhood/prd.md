# PRD: a neighbourhood around the plot

Source: [feature.md](feature.md) (approved by the Product Owner on 2026-11-09). Decisions: [decisions.md](decisions.md).

## Summary

The garden gets a neighbourhood. Next to our plot stand two neighbouring plots, and across the road stand five plots, all of our plot's size (24 m by 22 m). Each of the seven other plots has a fence, a closed gate, a tiled path, a Dutch or Belgian style house and a garden like ours, and each has one parked car at the curb and one street lamp. Our 3 cars become 1. A static picture of European buildings stands all around the horizon so that the neighbourhood seems to continue. All of it is scenery that cannot be reached.

## Problem

The garden stands alone on a long, mostly empty street: no neighbouring plot, no house across the road, a far sidewalk that leads to open grass, and 3 cars and 3 lamps grouped near our gate. A house alone in an empty landscape does not feel like a real, cosy home, which is what the place is meant to be.

How we know: one observation by the builder of the running app, together with the layout data of the street feature. No independent viewer has judged it and nothing has been measured. The Product Owner chose to go ahead on that basis.

## Users

- A person visiting the place (desktop browser, keyboard and mouse): walks around our garden and sees the street and the neighbourhood over the fence.
- The builder, who is also the Product Owner (A-1): judges every checklist item as met or not met.

## Goals and success metrics

- G-1: The garden sits inside a neighbourhood: a visitor who looks along the street or over the fence sees plots with houses on both sides of the road, in a European style. Metric: the number of the checklist items of R-1 to R-10, R-13 (its stand-in rows), R-14 and R-15 that the builder judges met (yes or no for each). Only the builder judges them. Baseline: 0, because no neighbouring plot, house or backdrop exists. Target: all items met, except up to 3 houses listed as missing because no free house model was found (R-3, R-14) and other items listed as missing (R-14) or reduced by the frame-rate rule, including fewer far plots (R-11); these exceptions are written down in the checklist. Timeframe: before the feature goes online.
- G-2: The place stays smooth. Metric: average frames per second over the 2-minute walk of R-11, read from the browser's built-in frame-rate display. Baseline: not yet measured with the street, the shadows and this addition. Target: 30 or more in Chrome on the builder's own laptop (named in the measurement); Firefox and Safari are best effort. Timeframe: before the feature goes online.
- G-3: The place stays within a download limit. Metric: compressed transfer size of the first visit with an empty cache, from the start screen through the place, the street, the neighbourhood, the house and the character, measured in Chrome's network tool with the person standing in the garden and no new file arriving for 10 seconds (R-12). Baseline: the shipped assets are about 16 MB today (measured from the shipped files on 2026-11-09); the first-visit transfer has not been measured. Target: at most 200 MB. Timeframe: before the feature goes online.
- G-4: The neighbourhood appears quickly after the place is ready. Metric: the time from the moment the place is ready (the loading indicator is gone) to the moment the whole neighbourhood is shown (every part has been replaced by its final model, picture or object, or is listed as missing, and no stand-in is left), measured in Chrome with no network throttling and an empty cache, on the builder's own connection, whose speed the builder names in the measurement. Baseline: not yet measured. Target: 60 seconds or less. On the Slow 4G profile there is no limit; the neighbourhood appears as it arrives. Timeframe: before the feature goes online.

## Non-goals

- Entering, opening or reaching any other plot, house, gate, car or lamp.
- Moving cars, pedestrians, traffic, animals, and any sound.
- Lit street lamps, night, weather and day-night changes.
- Changing our house, its interior, our garden, the character, the start screen or the intake.
- Interiors, people or characters in the other houses.
- Mobile and touch support.

## Requirements

### Functional

- R-1 (G-1): Directly next to our plot, on its left and on its right, stands one neighbouring plot of the same size as ours (24 m by 22 m). Both front edges lie on the same line as the front edge of our plot, along the street. Each neighbouring plot has a fence along all four edges, in the look of our fence (posts every metre, two rails, 1 m high, cream colour), a closed gate on the street side that cannot be opened and cannot be reached, and a tiled path of 1.2 m wide, built of 60 by 30 cm slabs like ours, from the gate to the front door of its house. The fence between our plot and a neighbouring plot is one shared fence line, not two fences side by side.
- R-2 (G-1): Across the road, on the far side, stand 5 plots of the same size, side by side along the street. The middle plot lies directly opposite our plot, with 2 plots on each side of it, so that the row is 120 m wide. The houses of the far plots face the road, and the street side of a far plot is the side of the far sidewalk. Each far plot has a fence along all four edges (shared between two neighbours as in R-1), a closed gate on the street side, which is the side of the far sidewalk, and a tiled path from the gate to its front door.
- R-3 (G-1): Each of the 7 other plots has one European-style house with a footprint of 8 m by 5 m, the same as our house, and 2 or 3 floors. The style is Dutch or Belgian: brick or rendered walls, tiled pitched or stepped-gable roofs, and large windows. The houses are not the same on every plot: they differ in facade colour or material, roof shape and number of floors, with at least 4 distinct looks among the houses. Each house faces the street, with its 8 m side toward the street as at our house, and its front door on the street side. A house counts as European-style when the builder answers yes to all of these: it has a pitched or stepped-gable roof with tiles, a brick or rendered facade, at least 4 windows facing the street and a front door on the street side. At least 4 houses are real models with distinct looks, of the 7 houses, or of the houses that remain after the frame-rate rule removed far plots (R-11), so that at most 3 of 7 (or at most 1 of 5) stand as plain blocks, listed as missing (R-14); if fewer real models are found, the feature does not go online. A house that stands as a plain block is listed as missing, is not judged by the style test and does not count as a look. The houses cannot be entered, and no interior exists. Our house, its interior and its look do not change.
- R-4 (G-1): Each of the 7 other plots has a garden with the same kinds of things as ours (trees, bushes, a hedge, a bench or table, flower beds), though fewer of each: at least 3 trees or bushes, 1 hedge, 1 bench or table and 1 flower bed. The positions differ per plot. Nothing stands on the path to the house, on the gate opening or on the house.
- R-5 (G-1): The 3 parked cars of today are reduced to 1 car at the curb in front of our plot. Each of the other 7 plots has 1 car as well, at the curb of its own side of the road, in front of that plot: 3 cars on our side (ours and the two neighbours') and 5 on the far side, 8 cars in total. Each car stands parallel to the curb, centred in front of its plot, facing the driving direction of its side of the road (right-hand traffic), so the cars on the far side face the opposite way to ours. The 8 cars show at least 3 different colours. Cars do not move and make no sound. The 8 cars may share a model, but no two cars of neighbouring plots on the same side of the road have the same colour.
- R-6 (G-1): The 3 street lamps are replaced by 1 street lamp per plot, standing on the sidewalk in front of its plot: 8 lamps in total. No lamp stands in the line of a gate opening or its path. The lamps on the far side stand on the far sidewalk. The lamps are not lit.
- R-7 (G-1): Behind the plots a static picture of European buildings stands on all sides of the horizon, so that the neighbourhood seems to continue in every direction. Our side shows one neighbouring plot on each side of ours (to x = ±36) and the far side 5 plots (to x = ±60); beyond the last plot on either side the grass continues to the backdrop. The picture is a band about 20 m high at a distance where the fog fades it. It is a picture and not a set of models: it does not move and has no depth. No seam, gap or end shows from anywhere in the garden. The existing grass ground continues in all directions up to the backdrop and is faded by the fog; no other objects stand between the last plots and the backdrop.
- R-8 (G-1): Everything of this feature (the other plots with their fences, gates, houses, gardens and paths, the cars, the lamps and the backdrop) is scenery that cannot be reached or entered. The person stays in our garden; fences and gates stop the person as they do today, and the closed gates mean the person cannot leave. The other plots, cars and lamps have no collision of their own, because they cannot be reached. The street keeps its dimensions (1.8 m sidewalks, 15 cm curbs, 7 m road) and carries on out of view.
- R-9 (G-1): These things keep working as before (the 3 cars and 3 lamps of the street feature are the one change, R-5 and R-6, and replace the earlier criteria about them): walking and looking, solid objects and our fence stopping the person, our front door and the enterable house, the character with his animations, colours, wandering, greeting and waving, the sun and its shadows, the start screen and the intake. The walkable area outside the house on our plot stays at least 444.5 m². Our spawn point, our path and our gate stay where they are. The shadows of our plot, our house and the character are not visibly softer than today, judged by the builder by comparing the same view before and after, side by side. This outranks the shadows of the neighbourhood: shadows on the other plots and the street are softer or absent where that keeps the shadows of our plot as they are.
- R-10 (G-1): The new objects (fences, gates, houses, garden objects, cars, lamps) cast and receive sun shadows like the other objects in the place. The backdrop picture does not. Shadows on the other plots and the street may be softer than on our plot; they may also be dropped by the frame-rate rule (R-11). The taller houses of the neighbours may cast shadows on our garden as the sun dictates; that is expected.
- R-13 (G-4): Our plot and the street (its sidewalks, curbs and road, without the cars and lamps) keep the 30-second loading rule of the earlier features on a Slow 4G connection; a stand-in for a car or lamp counts as loaded for that rule. The neighbourhood (the other plots, their houses and gardens, all cars and lamps including our own car and lamp, and the backdrop) loads after the place is ready and appears as it arrives. Until a part has arrived, a plain block or colour stands in its place, as for the interior of the house, at the final position and size of the part. A delayed, a deliberate (R-14) and a failed (R-15) stand-in look the same. With no network throttling, the whole neighbourhood is shown within 60 seconds of the place being ready; on Slow 4G there is no time limit.
- R-15 (G-1): If an asset of this feature fails to load (not when a plain block is used on purpose under R-14, which gives no message), the place still loads, with a plain block or colour in its place and a short message of one sentence in the place's existing notice area, shown the same way as other load messages. At most one such message is shown per visit, however many assets fail and whenever they fail. The fences and gates do not depend on a loaded file.

### Non-functional

- R-11 (G-2): Walking averages 30 frames per second or better over the same 2-minute walk as the street feature: from the start spot along the path to the front door, through every room, and back out. The frame rate is read from the browser's built-in frame-rate display every 10 seconds, and the average of the 12 values is the measurement. If the builder's measurement misses the target, the following steps are taken one after the other, and the walk is measured again after each step: step 1, the garden objects and the cars of the 7 other plots are removed (one step); step 2, the sun shadows on the new objects are dropped; step 3, the 5 plots across the road are reduced to 3 (the middle one and one on each side). A removed far plot disappears completely (its fence, gate, path, house, garden, car and lamp) and its space is grass. Step 1 removes only the garden objects and the cars; the fences, gates, paths, houses and lamps of the other plots stay until step 3. The checklist rows of R-2, R-3, R-4, R-5, R-6 and R-7 are then judged on what remains, and the steps taken are written down in the checklist. Our plot, the street and the backdrop are never reduced. This is decided before the feature goes online, from the builder's measurement; nothing switches at run time. If the walk still misses the target after step 3, the feature does not go online until it is met.
- R-12 (G-3): The compressed first-visit transfer, with an empty cache, from the start screen through the place, the street, the neighbourhood, the house and the character, is at most 200 MB. It is read with the person standing in the garden, once no new file has arrived for 10 seconds.
- R-14 (G-1): Every new asset (house models, textures, garden objects, cars, lamps and the backdrop picture) has a recorded licence (author, source link, licence text, any credit line) that is usable: CC0, CC-BY with the credit shown, or one that explicitly allows use and redistribution. NC, ND or no licence is not usable. Credit lines are shown in the existing Credits list of the start page. Every asset ships with the app and loads from its own address, never from a third-party host. The car models and textures carry no readable brand logos or number plates. The builder must first try to find a free asset for each item. If none can be found, three rules apply. First, the houses: R-3 decides how many must be real models. Second, the backdrop: it must be a real picture, a free licensed picture from a source with a usable licence, recorded like every other asset; generated images are not allowed; if none is found, the feature does not go online. The picture shows no brand name or sign that is readable, and no recognisable people, when the builder views it at full size on screen. Third, every other asset: a plain block or colour in its place is acceptable, the feature still ships and the missing items are listed.
- R-16 (G-1): Nothing about a person is stored or sent. This feature collects, stores and sends no personal data.
- R-17 (G-1): Each of R-1 to R-10, R-13, R-14 and R-15 has a short written checklist of concrete things to see, written into the spec's acceptance criteria before building starts, so that the builder can judge it as met or not met. R-11, R-12 and the time limit of R-13 are measured conditions, not checklist items, and R-16 and R-17 are standing conditions of the whole feature.

## Scope and out of scope

In scope: 7 neighbouring plots of the size of ours: one on each side of our plot and 5 across the road, with fences, closed gates and tiled paths; One European-style house per plot (8 m by 5 m, 2 or 3 floors, at least 4 distinct looks) and a smaller garden like ours; One car per plot at the curb (our 3 cars become 1, 8 in total) and one lamp per plot (8 in total); A static picture of European buildings around the horizon; Sun shadows on the new objects, licence records and credits, plain blocks and colours for missing assets, and loading after the place is ready; The one change to the street of the street feature: its 3 cars and 3 lamps become 1 car and 1 lamp per plot (R-5, R-6); the earlier criteria about the 3 cars and 3 lamps no longer apply and their checklist rows are updated.

Out of scope: see Non-goals.

## Assumptions

- A-1: The builder and the PO are the same person, as in the earlier features. Only the builder judges the checklists.
- A-2: Our plot is 24 m by 22 m (x from −12 to 12, z from −11 to 11), and its front edge (z = 11) lies along the street. The street runs from the front fence to the far sidewalk: sidewalk 1.8 m (z 11 to 12.8), curb, road 7 m (z 13 to 20), far curb and far sidewalk 1.8 m (ending at z = 22). Measured from the layout data on 2026-11-09 (street feature and its far-sidewalk change, decision D-27 there).
- A-3: The 7 other plots are 24 m by 22 m. The neighbouring plots on our side lie at x from −36 to −12 and from 12 to 36. The 5 far plots lie at x from −60 to 60 and z from 22 to 44, with their front edge on the far sidewalk. The street already runs out of view on both sides.
- A-4: Free models with usable licences exist for Dutch or Belgian style houses, for garden objects (our trees and bushes are reused), and a free licensed picture for the backdrop. Not yet sourced. R-14 allows plain blocks or colours if none is found.
- A-5: The existing 3 car models and the lamp model may be reused for the 8 cars and 8 lamps, as long as no two cars of neighbouring plots on the same side of the road share a colour (R-5).
- A-6: Whether the houses are separate models or built from parts, and how the backdrop picture is made, are technical choices for the plan, as long as R-3 and R-7 hold.

## Dependencies and risks

- Risk: 7 houses, 7 gardens, 8 cars and 8 lamps cost frame rate and download size. Addressed by: R-11, R-12 and R-13. The limit of 200 MB is about 12 times what ships today and twice the earlier 100 MB limit; a visitor on a slow connection waits much longer for the neighbourhood, which is why R-13 loads it after the place is ready.
- Risk: the shadow box of the sun (a sphere around the place) must hold the neighbourhood; a larger scene lowers the sharpness of shadows. Addressed by: R-10 (the technical plan decides how far shadows reach).
- Risk: licence work grows with the number of asset types (houses, garden objects, cars, lamps, backdrop); every CC-BY credit adds to the one Credits list on the start page. Desktop browsers need enough graphics memory for the larger scene; this is measured only on the builder's laptop (R-11), as in the street feature.
- Risk: the other plots reuse our garden objects (trees, bushes), so the 7 gardens may look alike. Addressed by: R-4 (positions differ per plot).
- Dependency: the street feature (`specs/garden-fences-and-street/`: fence, gate, slab path, street, cars, lamps, shadow region; the shadow settings are tied to the size of the place and its tests must hold for the larger scene), the asset-size check of the earlier features (changed to fit the 200 MB first-visit limit), the existing Credits list and licence checks (R-14), the loading rule for late parts (R-13).
- Compliance: no personal data is involved and nothing is stored or sent, so the GDPR and the EU AI Act are not touched (R-16). The backdrop picture must carry no readable brand names, signs or recognisable people (R-14). This is an attention point, not a legal opinion.
