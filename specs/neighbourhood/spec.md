# OpenFreeyond: a neighbourhood around the plot: Spec

## Definitions

- Place: the 3D area a person walks around in, as defined in the phase 1 spec ([spec.md](../openfreeyond-companion/spec.md)).
- Our plot: the fenced garden with our house, 24 m by 22 m, as built by the street feature ([spec.md](../garden-fences-and-street/spec.md)). The person stays in it.
- Street side: the edge of a plot that faces the road. For our plot and the plots next to it it is the front edge along the street; for the far plots it is the edge along the far sidewalk.
- Street: the sidewalks, curbs and road beyond the front fence of our plot, as built by the street feature (sidewalk 1.8 m, curb 15 cm, road 7 m with a centre line, a far curb and a far sidewalk of 1.8 m), without the cars and lamps. The cars and lamps belong to the neighbourhood.
- Neighbouring plots: the two plots of the same size as ours that lie directly next to it, one on its left and one on its right, with their front edges on the same line as the front edge of our plot.
- Far plots: the 5 plots of the same size as ours that lie across the road, side by side along the far sidewalk. The middle one lies directly opposite our plot.
- Other plots: the 2 neighbouring plots and the 5 far plots, 7 in all.
- Neighbourhood: the other plots with their fences, gates, paths, houses and gardens, all cars and lamps including the one car and one lamp in front of our plot, and the backdrop.
- Scenery: something the person can see but cannot reach, enter or touch. The person is stopped by the fence of our plot.
- Fence look: posts about every metre, two rails, 1 m high, cream colour, as the fence of our plot.
- Closed gate: a gate in the street-side fence that matches the fence in look and height, has a clear opening of 1.2 m between its two posts, is centred on the path of its plot, and cannot be opened.
- Path: the way of paving slabs of 60 by 30 cm, 1.2 m wide, from the closed gate to the front door of a house, with whole slabs along its long sides. The gate and the front door are in line, so the path runs straight.
- Gate line: the strip as wide as the gate opening (1.2 m), from the front door of a house along its path to the street side of the plot, and on across the sidewalk to the curb.
- House: the one building on an other plot, with a footprint of 8 m by 5 m, 2 or 3 floors, the 8 m side toward the street, and a front door on the street side. Not enterable.
- Real model: a house, car, lamp or garden object that is drawn from a model made for it, not a plain block.
- Plain block: a simple block or colour that stands in the place of an asset for which no free asset was found, or whose file failed to load. When none was found, it is listed as missing.
- Stand-in: the plain block or colour that stands at the final position and size of a part until the part has arrived (a delayed stand-in), or in place of a part that is missing or failed. Its position and size are in the layout data; for a house it is the footprint and a height of 2.5 m per floor.
- Look: the combination of facade colour or material, roof shape and number of floors of a house. Two looks are distinct when the facade colour is a different hue or the material differs (not only a different shade), or the roof shape differs, or the number of floors differs. A plain block does not have a look.
- Style test: the builder answers yes to all four of these for a house: it has a pitched or stepped-gable roof with tiles, a brick or rendered facade, at least 4 windows facing the street, and a front door on the street side.
- Backdrop: the static picture of European buildings that stands about 20 m high around the horizon, behind the plots. It has no moving parts.
- Place ready: the moment the loading indicator is gone and our plot and the street are shown.
- Neighbourhood shown: the moment every part of the neighbourhood has been replaced by its final model, picture or object, or is listed as missing, so that no stand-in remains that is waiting for a file.
- Frame-rate walk: a 2-minute walk that always follows the same route: from the start spot along the path to the front door, through every room of the house and back out. The walk starts as soon as the place is ready, while the neighbourhood is still arriving, as it does for a visitor.
- Frame-rate steps: the three steps of FR-13, taken one after the other until a measurement meets the target.
- Layout data: the written list of the positions and sizes of everything in the place, which the builder can read.
- Load message: a text of one short sentence that tells the person that something failed to load, shown in the place's existing notice area.
- Missing items: the list, in the checklist, of items for which no free asset was found.
- Test note: the note in which the builder writes down a measurement, with the laptop, the browser and its version, the operating system and the speed of the connection.
- Try-out: the option on the start screen that enters the place with the example character, with no account or personal details.
- Builder: the person who builds the place and judges the checklists. The builder is also the Product Owner (assumption A-1).
- Checklist: the acceptance criteria below. The builder judges each one as met or not met, yes or no.

## Goal and Success Criteria

The garden sits inside a neighbourhood. Success is measured by the goals of the feature document ([feature.md](feature.md)):

- G-1: The garden sits inside a neighbourhood: plots with European-style houses on both sides of the road. Baseline: 0, because no neighbouring plot, house or backdrop exists. Measured by the checklist items AC-1.1 to AC-1.9, AC-2.1 to AC-2.9, AC-3.1 to AC-3.7, AC-4.1 to AC-4.5, AC-4.7, AC-4.8, AC-5.1 to AC-5.6, AC-6.7, AC-6.9, AC-6.10 to AC-6.18, AC-6.20 that the builder judges met. Target: all met, except up to 3 houses listed as missing (FR-5), other items listed as missing (FR-15), and items reduced or dropped by the frame-rate steps (FR-13); these exceptions are written down in the checklist. Before the feature goes online. Only the builder judges them.
- G-2: The place stays smooth. Baseline: not yet measured with the street, the shadows and this addition. Measured by NFR-1, with the checklist items AC-6.1 to AC-6.4. If the target is missed, FR-13 applies.
- G-3: The place stays within a download limit. Baseline: shipped assets about 16 MB (measured 2026-11-09), first-visit transfer not measured. Measured by NFR-2, with the checklist item AC-6.5.
- G-4: The neighbourhood appears quickly after the place is ready. Baseline: not yet measured. Measured by NFR-3, with the checklist items AC-6.6 and AC-6.8.

AC-6.19, AC-7.1 and AC-7.2 are standing conditions that hold for all four goals.

## User Stories

- US-1: As a visitor, I want plots next to mine and across the road, each fenced, with a closed gate and a path, so that my garden is one of many on a real street, even though I cannot go there.
- US-2: As a visitor, I want a European-style house and a garden on every other plot, with differences from plot to plot, so that the neighbourhood looks lived in and varied.
- US-3: As a visitor, I want one parked car and one street lamp for every plot, so that the street feels in use and not staged.
- US-4: As a visitor, I want a picture of more buildings on the horizon, so that the neighbourhood seems to continue past the last plot.
- US-5: As a visitor, I want everything that worked before to keep working, with my own shadows as sharp as before and shadows on the new objects, so that the new scenery takes nothing away.
- US-6: As the builder, I want the place to stay smooth, within a download limit, quick to show, with only licensed assets and a fallback when something is missing or fails, so that the feature can go online.
- US-7: As a visitor, I want nothing about me to be stored or sent, so that my visit stays private.

## Functional Requirements

- FR-1: Directly next to our plot stands one neighbouring plot on its left and one on its right. Each is 24 m by 22 m and its front edge lies on the same line as the front edge of our plot. Story: US-1.
- FR-2: Across the road stand 5 far plots, each 24 m by 22 m, side by side along the far sidewalk, so that the row is 120 m wide. The middle far plot lies directly opposite our plot, with 2 far plots on each side of it. Our side shows one neighbouring plot on each side of ours (to x = ±36 from the centre of our plot) and the far side shows 5 plots (to x = ±60). Beyond the last plot on either side the ground continues to the backdrop. Story: US-1.
- FR-3: Each of the 7 other plots has a fence along all four edges in the fence look, a closed gate on its street side, and a path from the gate to the front door of its house. The fence between two plots that touch (our plot and a neighbouring plot, or two far plots) is one shared fence line, not two fences side by side. The gates cannot be opened and cannot be reached. The position of the gate, path and front door differs per plot, with the nearest edge of the clear opening of the gate at least 1.2 m from the corners of the plot. Story: US-1.
- FR-4: Each of the 7 other plots has one house. The house has a footprint of 8 m by 5 m, the same as our house, with the 8 m side toward the street, 2 or 3 floors, and a front door on the street side; the houses of the far plots face the road. The style is Dutch or Belgian and every real house passes the style test. The houses differ in facade colour or material, roof shape and number of floors, with at least 4 distinct looks among the real houses. The position of the house differs per plot: it stands at least 3 m behind the street-side fence and at least 1 m from the other fences, with its front door in line with the gate. A house cannot be entered and has no interior. Our house, its interior and its look do not change. Story: US-2.
- FR-5: At least 4 houses are real models with distinct looks, among the 7 houses, or among the houses that remain after the frame-rate steps removed far plots (FR-13). Up to 3 of 7 (or 1 of 5) may stand as plain blocks, listed as missing, and a plain block is not judged by the style test and does not count as a look. If fewer real models are found, the feature does not go online. Story: US-2.
- FR-6: Each of the 7 other plots has a garden with the same kinds of things as ours: at least 3 trees or bushes, 1 hedge, 1 bench or table and 1 flower bed. The positions differ per plot. Nothing stands on the gate line or on the house. Story: US-2.
- FR-7: Our 3 parked cars become 1 car at the curb in front of our plot. Each of the other 7 plots has 1 car at the curb of its own side of the road, in front of it: 3 cars on our side and 5 on the far side, 8 cars in all. Each car stands parallel to the curb, centred in front of its plot, facing the driving direction of its side of the road (right-hand traffic), so the cars on the far side face the opposite way to ours. The 8 cars show at least 3 different colours, and no two cars of neighbouring plots on the same side of the road have the same colour. The cars do not move and make no sound. Story: US-3.
- FR-8: There is one street lamp for each plot, 8 in all, on the sidewalk in front of its plot: the lamps of our side on our sidewalk and the lamps of the far side on the far sidewalk. No lamp stands on a gate line. The lamps do not move, make no sound and are not lit. The 3 lamps of the street feature are replaced by these. Story: US-3.
- FR-9: A static picture of European buildings stands on all sides of the horizon behind the plots, as a band about 20 m high (between 15 m and 25 m) at a distance where the fog fades it. It does not move and has no depth. No seam, gap or end of the picture shows, and no void between the ground and the sky, from anywhere in the garden. The grass ground continues up to the backdrop, and only grass and the street (which runs on until the fog hides it) lie between the last plots and the backdrop. Until the picture has arrived, or if it failed to load, a plain band in the colour of the horizon stands at its place, so that no void shows. The buildings in the picture show pitched or stepped-gable tiled roofs, brick or rendered facades and windows (the traits of the style test that a distant picture can show). The picture is either a photograph from a free licensed source or a picture generated with an image tool, both under FR-15; if neither is available, the feature does not go online. The picture shows no readable brand name or sign and no recognisable people when viewed at full size on screen. Story: US-4.
- FR-10: Everything of this feature (the other plots with their fences, gates, houses, gardens and paths, the cars, the lamps and the backdrop) is scenery. The person stays in our garden, stopped by the fence of our plot, and the closed gates mean the person cannot leave. The street keeps its dimensions and carries on out of view at both ends. Story: US-1.
- FR-11: These keep working as before: walking and looking, our fence and gate stopping the person, our front door and the enterable house, the character with his animations, colours, wandering, greeting and waving, the sun and its shadows, the start screen and the intake. The walkable area outside the house on our plot stays at least 444.5 m2. Our spawn point, our path and our gate stay where they are. The shadows of our plot, our house and the character are not visibly softer than today. The only change to existing behaviour is the one car and one lamp in front of our plot, which replace the 3 cars and 3 lamps of the street feature. Story: US-5.
- FR-12: The new objects (fences, gates, houses, garden objects, cars, lamps) cast and receive sun shadows like the other objects in the place. The backdrop does not. Shadows on the other plots and the street may be softer or absent where that keeps the shadows of our plot as they are. The taller houses of the neighbours may cast shadows on our garden as the sun dictates. Story: US-5.
- FR-13: If the builder's measurement misses the target of NFR-1, three steps are taken one after the other, and the walk is measured again after each step. Step 1: the garden objects and the cars of the 7 other plots are removed. Step 2: the sun shadows on the new objects are dropped. Step 3: the 5 far plots are reduced to 3 (the middle one and the inner one on each side, so the row stays unbroken). The steps stop at the first measurement that meets the target of NFR-1. A removed far plot disappears completely (its fence, gate, path, house, garden, car and lamp) and its space is grass. Step 1 removes only the garden objects and the cars; the fences, gates, paths, houses and lamps stay until step 3. Our plot, the street and the backdrop are never reduced. The checklist rows of FR-2, FR-3, FR-4, FR-6, FR-7 and FR-8 are then judged on the plots that remain, and the rows of FR-12 on what is left after step 2, and the steps taken are written down in the checklist. This is decided before the feature goes online, from the builder's measurement; nothing switches at run time. If the walk still misses the target after step 3, the feature does not go online until it is met. Story: US-6.
- FR-14: Our plot and the street keep the 30-second loading rule of the earlier features on a Slow 4G connection; a stand-in for a car or lamp counts as loaded for that rule. The neighbourhood, including our own car and lamp, loads after the place is ready and appears as it arrives. Until a part has arrived, a stand-in stands at its final position and size. A delayed, a deliberate and a failed stand-in look the same. On Slow 4G the neighbourhood has no time limit. Story: US-6.
- FR-15: Every new asset (house models, textures, garden objects, cars, lamps and the backdrop picture) has a recorded licence with author, source link, licence text and any credit line, and the licence is usable: CC0, CC-BY with the credit shown, or one that explicitly allows use and redistribution. NC, ND or no licence is not usable. Credit lines are shown in the existing Credits list of the start page. Every asset ships with the app and loads from its own address, never from a third-party host. The car models and textures carry no readable brand logos or number plates. The builder first tries to find a free asset for each item. If none is found, the houses follow FR-5, the backdrop follows FR-9, and for every other asset a plain block or colour in its place is acceptable: the feature still ships, and the item is on the list of missing items with what was tried. A picture generated with an image tool is allowed only for the backdrop: its record names the tool, the terms of the tool must allow use and redistribution of the output, and the builder confirms this before the feature goes online. Story: US-6.
- FR-16: If an asset of this feature fails to load, the place still loads, with a plain block or colour in its place and a load message. At most one load message is shown per visit, however many assets fail and whenever they fail. A plain block that stands there on purpose (FR-15) gives no message. The fences and gates do not depend on a loaded file. Story: US-6.

## Non-Functional Requirements

- NFR-1: The average frame rate over a frame-rate walk is 30 frames per second or better in Chrome on the builder's own laptop, which the builder names in the test note. The frame rate is read from the browser's built-in frame-rate display every 10 seconds during the walk, and the average of those 12 values is the measurement. Firefox and Safari are best effort. No floor is promised on other laptops. Goal: G-2.
- NFR-2: The compressed first-visit transfer, with an empty cache, from the start screen through the place, the street, the neighbourhood, the house and the character, is at most 200 MB. The measurement is read with the person standing in the garden, once no new file has arrived for 10 seconds. This limit replaces the 100 MB limit of the earlier features. Goal: G-3.
- NFR-3: With no network throttling, the whole neighbourhood is shown within 60 seconds of the place being ready, measured in Chrome with an empty cache on the builder's own connection, whose speed the builder names in the test note. Goal: G-4.
- NFR-4: Nothing about a person is stored or sent. The feature collects, stores and sends no personal data. Loading the place and its files from its web address does not count as data leaving the browser. Story: US-7.
- NFR-5: The neighbourhood works in the latest stable Chrome, Firefox and Safari on desktop. The versions and the operating system are recorded in the test note. Only the frame-rate target of NFR-1 is limited to Chrome. Story: US-6.
- NFR-6: No accessibility level is promised for this feature, as for the rest of the product. The place works with keyboard and mouse or trackpad only, on desktop browsers (assumption A-6). This is a known limitation. Story: US-6.

## Acceptance Criteria

Each criterion is a short checklist item that the builder judges as met or not met. Requirements FR-1 to FR-16 are verified through the user stories they serve. AC-6.19, AC-7.1 and AC-7.2 are standing conditions that hold for all four goals. When the frame-rate steps of FR-13 removed far plots, garden objects or cars, the criteria about them are judged on what remains, and a criterion that cannot apply after a step is not judged and is listed in the checklist. NFR-6 is a stated limitation and has no criterion.

- AC-1.1: Given the layout data, when the builder reads the plots next to ours, then one plot of 24 m by 22 m lies on each side of ours, at x from -36 to -12 and from 12 to 36, with its front edge on the same line as the front edge of our plot.
- AC-1.2: Given the layout data, when the builder reads the far plots, then 5 plots of 24 m by 22 m lie side by side along the far sidewalk, from x = -60 to x = 60, and the middle one lies directly opposite our plot.
- AC-1.3: Given the person looks at each of the 7 other plots from our garden or along the street, when the builder asks "does it have a fence along all four edges, with posts about every metre, two rails, 1 m high and cream colour?", then the answer is yes for each.
- AC-1.4: Given the boundary between our plot and a neighbouring plot, and between two far plots, when the builder looks at it, then one fence line stands there and not two fences side by side.
- AC-1.5: Given each of the 7 other plots, when the builder looks at its street side, then a closed gate with a clear opening of 1.2 m stands there, centred on the path of that plot, matching the fence in look and height.
- AC-1.6: Given each of the 7 other plots, when the builder looks along its path, then the path runs from its gate to the front door of its house, is 1.2 m wide, is made of 60 by 30 cm slabs with visible joints, and shows no partial slab along its long sides.
- AC-1.7: Given the person walks along all four edges of our plot, and into every part of our fence and our gate, when the person keeps moving, then the person never leaves our garden and never reaches another plot, and a fence of our plot is directly in front of the person each time the person stops.
- AC-1.8: Given the person clicks on a gate, a house door, a car or a lamp of the neighbourhood, when the builder watches, then nothing opens, and nothing moves or changes shape or position.
- AC-1.9: Given the person walks to each end of the front fence of our plot and looks along the street in both directions, when the builder asks "does the road with its sidewalks carry on out of view, with no end in sight?", then the answer is yes, and the sidewalks are 1.8 m, the curbs 15 cm and the road 7 m, as before.
- AC-2.1: Given the layout data, when the builder reads each house, then its footprint is 8 m by 5 m, it has 2 or 3 floors, and its front door is on the street side.
- AC-2.2: Given each real house, when the builder applies the style test, then the answer is yes: a pitched or stepped-gable roof with tiles, a brick or rendered facade, at least 4 windows facing the street, and a front door on the street side.
- AC-2.3: Given the real houses, when the builder compares them, then at least 4 distinct looks exist, differing in facade colour or material, roof shape or number of floors.
- AC-2.4: Given the 7 houses, or the houses that remain after far plots were removed, when the builder counts the real models, then at least 4 are real models with distinct looks, and each plain block is on the list of missing items. If fewer than 4 are real models, the feature is not put online.
- AC-2.5: Given the person looks at the other houses from our garden, when the builder asks "can any of them be entered, or does any door open?", then the answer is no, and no interior shows through any window.
- AC-2.6: Given our house before and after the change, when the builder compares its outside, its door, its interior and its look, then nothing has changed.
- AC-2.7: Given each of the 7 gardens, when the builder counts what stands in it, then it has at least 3 trees or bushes, 1 hedge, 1 bench or table and 1 flower bed, the positions are not the same on any two plots, and nothing stands on the gate line or on the house.
- AC-2.8: Given the layout data, when the builder reads the 7 houses, then the 8 m side of each house is toward the street, the houses of the far plots face the road and the houses of our side face the street.
- AC-2.9: Given the layout data, when the builder reads the gate, path, front door and house of each of the 7 plots, then on each plot the gate and the front door are in line, the nearest edge of the clear opening of the gate is at least 1.2 m from the corners of the plot, the house stands at least 3 m behind the street-side fence and at least 1 m from the other fences, and no two plots have the same gate position and the same house position.
- AC-3.1: Given the street in front of our plot, when the builder counts the cars at the curb in front of it, then exactly 1 car stands there, and the 3 cars of the earlier street stand no more.
- AC-3.2: Given the layout data, when the builder counts the cars, then each of the 7 other plots has 1 car at the curb of its own side of the road in front of it, centred on its width and parallel to the curb: 3 on our side including ours and 5 on the far side, 8 in all, except cars removed by step 1 of FR-13.
- AC-3.3: Given the cars of our side and of the far side, when the builder looks at which way they face, then each faces the driving direction of its side (right-hand traffic) and the cars on the far side face the opposite way to ours, except cars removed by step 1 of FR-13.
- AC-3.4: Given the 8 cars, when the builder looks at the main body colour of each as the builder names it, then at least 3 different colours show, and no two cars of neighbouring plots on the same side of the road have the same colour; after step 1 of FR-13 has removed the cars of the other plots this criterion is not judged.
- AC-3.5: Given the layout data, when the builder counts the lamps, then there are 8, one in front of each plot on the sidewalk (the far lamps on the far sidewalk), and none stands on a gate line.
- AC-3.6: Given the person watches the street for 1 minute, when the builder looks at the cars and the lamps, then nothing moves, no sound is played, and the lamps are not lit.
- AC-3.7: Given the cars and lamps that are real models, when the builder looks at each, then the answer to "is it recognisable as a car, or as a street lamp?" is yes. An item that stands as a plain block is on the list of missing items and is not counted.
- AC-4.1: Given the person stands in the middle of our garden and turns all the way round, when the builder looks at the horizon, then a picture of buildings shows on all sides behind the plots, and the builder answers yes to "do the buildings show pitched or stepped-gable tiled roofs, brick or rendered facades and windows?".
- AC-4.2: Given the person walks to each edge of our garden and looks out over the fence, including along the ends of the street, when the builder asks "is there any seam, gap or end of the picture, or any void between the ground and the sky?", then the answer is no.
- AC-4.3: Given the person looks from our garden toward the backdrop, when the builder looks at what lies between the last plots and the picture, then only grass ground and the street show, faded by the fog.
- AC-4.4: Given the person walks around our garden for 1 minute, when the builder looks at the picture, then it does not move or change, and its height is between 15 m and 25 m in the layout data.
- AC-4.5: Given the picture is viewed at full size on screen, when the builder looks for brand names, signs and people, then none is readable or recognisable.
- AC-4.7: Given the backdrop picture has not arrived yet or failed to load, when the builder looks at the horizon, then a plain band in the colour of the horizon stands at its place and no void shows between the ground and the sky.
- AC-4.8: Given neither a free licensed photograph nor a generated picture whose terms allow use and redistribution is available for the backdrop, when the builder decides about going online, then the feature is not put online.
- AC-5.1: Given the checklists of the earlier specs for the start screen, the intake, the character's animations and colours, walking and looking, the front door and the house, the street and the sun shadows, when the builder goes through them, then each is still met, except the criteria that this feature changes on purpose: the 3 cars and the 3 lamps of the street feature (FR-9 and AC-4.4 there). The builder lists in the checklist each criterion judged as changed on purpose.
- AC-5.2: Given the layout data, when the builder measures the walkable area outside the house on our plot, with the fence's footprint counted as covered and the method of the phase 1 comparison, then it is at least 444.5 m2.
- AC-5.3: Given a new visit, when the person enters the place, then the person stands where they stood before this feature: on the path 2 m inside our gate, facing the house, and our path and our gate are where they were.
- AC-5.4: Given the front door is open, when the builder watches the character wander for 5 minutes, then he stays in our garden inside the fence; and given a person comes within 3 metres of him, then he stops, faces the person and waves once, as before.
- AC-5.5: Given the same view of our plot, our house and the character before and after the change, when the builder compares the two side by side, then the shadows are not visibly softer after the change. The view before is captured from the build before this feature changes the scene.
- AC-5.6: Given the sun and its shadows, when the builder looks at the fences, gates, houses, garden objects, cars and lamps of the other plots, then each casts a visible shadow, except where step 2 of FR-13 dropped them. The shadows they show on other things may be softer or absent as FR-12 allows and are not judged. The backdrop casts and shows none.
- AC-6.1: Given Chrome on the builder's own laptop and the frame-rate display on, when the builder does a frame-rate walk and notes the displayed value every 10 seconds, then the average of the 12 values is 30 frames per second or better, and the laptop is named in the test note.
- AC-6.2: Given the builder's measurement misses 30 frames per second, when the builder decides about going online, then the steps of FR-13 were taken one after the other, each followed by a new measurement, and stopped at the first measurement that met 30 frames per second, never reducing our plot, the street or the backdrop, and the steps taken are written in the checklist.
- AC-6.3: Given step 3 removed far plots, when the builder looks at the street, then each removed far plot is gone completely, with its fence, gate, path, house, garden, car and lamp, and its space is grass, and the criteria about the other plots are judged on what remains.

- AC-6.12: Given the network tool is open during a visit, when the builder inspects the requests, then every request for a file of the place goes to the place's own web address and none goes to a third-party host.

## Dependencies and Risks

- Dependency: the street feature ([spec.md](../garden-fences-and-street/spec.md)): the fence, the gate, the slab path, the street, the cars and lamps, and the shadow region. Its criteria about the 3 cars and 3 lamps are replaced by FR-7 and FR-8.
- Dependency: the existing Credits list and licence checks (FR-15), the loading rule for late parts of the earlier features (FR-14), the sun-shadow work already in the place (FR-12).
- Dependency: the asset-size check of the earlier features, which is changed to fit the 200 MB first-visit limit (NFR-2).
- Dependency: the shadow settings are tied to the size of the place, and their tests must hold for the larger scene (FR-11, FR-12).
- Risk: 7 houses, 7 gardens, 8 cars and 8 lamps cost frame rate and download size. Addressed by: NFR-1, FR-13 and NFR-2. The limit of 200 MB is about 12 times what ships today and twice the earlier 100 MB limit.
- Risk: a visitor on a slow connection waits much longer for the neighbourhood. Addressed by: FR-14.
- Risk: a larger scene lowers the sharpness of the sun's shadows on our plot. Addressed by: FR-11 and FR-12.
- Risk: licence work grows with the number of asset types, and every CC-BY credit adds to the one Credits list on the start page. Addressed by: FR-15.
- Risk: desktop browsers need enough graphics memory for the larger scene; it is measured only on the builder's laptop. Not addressed (decision: the same basis as the street feature; no floor on other laptops, NFR-1).
- Risk: the frame-rate walk starts at place ready, so its first part may show fewer objects than the shipped scene. Not addressed (decision: the Product Owner chose to start the walk at place ready, D-35).
- Risk: the 7 gardens reuse our garden objects and may look alike. Addressed by: FR-6.
- Risk: no free Dutch or Belgian style house models are found. Addressed by: FR-5.
- Risk: no usable backdrop picture is available. Addressed by: FR-9.
- Risk: no personal data is involved and nothing is stored or sent, so the GDPR and the EU AI Act are not touched. Addressed by: NFR-4. The backdrop must show no recognisable people and no readable brand names or signs (FR-9). This is an attention point, not a legal opinion.
