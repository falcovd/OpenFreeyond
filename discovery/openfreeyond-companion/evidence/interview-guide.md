# Interview guide: OpenFreeyond

## Participant profile
Adult (roughly 25-45) who uses a desktop or laptop and enjoys relaxed games or screen-based hobbies (cozy, exploration or story games, or AI chat/voice tools). Not a developer of this project. Friends and colleagues are fine; do not pick people you expect to agree.
**Minimum: 3 interviews** (threshold from status.md). Aim for people who differ in how much they game.

## Before you start
- Anonymize notes (no names). Ask consent before any recording.
- Do not describe or pitch OpenFreeyond. Do not mention a virtual world with a talking character until the last question.
- Listen for what they did, not what they think they would do.

## Questions
1. Tell me about the last time you spent free time on a game or app just to relax. What did you do, and how long?
2. How often does that happen in a normal week? (A1)
3. Tell me about a game or world you went back to again and again. What kept you there? What was missing, if anything? (A1, A2)
4. Tell me about the last time you tried a chatbot, voice assistant or companion app for something other than a task. What happened? (A2, A5)
5. Think of the last time you wanted something relaxing or social to do and found nothing that fit. What did you do instead? (A2, A3, A4)
6. When that happens, what usually gets in the way: price, time, access, or something about the experience itself? (A3)
7. Tell me about a time you felt a bit alone or bored in the evening. What did you do, and did anything help? (A4)
8. Have you ever talked out loud to a character in a game, or to a voice assistant, beyond giving commands? Tell me about it. (A5)
9. Tell me about a time you made a space (a room, a game base, a profile) feel like yours. What did you do and why? (A2, A3)
10. (Last, only after the above) Imagine a cozy browser world where you walk around and a fictional person lives there, and you could chat by voice. What is your first reaction? What would put you off? (A5, A2)

## Disproving questions
Questions 3, 5, 6 and 7 can show satisfaction with existing options (A2), other impediments (A3) or no frustration (A4). Question 10 can show A5 does not hold. Do not steer people away from negative answers.

## Interview note template
Save each interview as its own file in `evidence/interviews/` (for example `interview-01.md`).

```
# Interview <nr>: <date>
Participant: <role, context; no name>
Matches persona: yes / partly / no (why)
Context of the conversation: <short>

## Observed behavior (what they did, not what they think)
## Verbatim quotes (exact words, with a short note on context)
## Emotions or frustration expressed
## Assumptions touched: A1 / A2 / ... (supports, contradicts or unclear)
## Surprises
```

Free-form notes are also fine; they will be mapped onto this structure.
